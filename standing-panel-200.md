# Dental Focus Group Panel — 200 personas in two benches

The standing panel for the focus-group skill: 200 fictional dental-industry personas in two benches. Bench A (seats 1–100) was built to the skill's roster rules from scratch; its dentist seats mirror the ADA Health Policy Institute workforce table. Bench B (seats 101–200) was merged in from a 100-row roster built on another platform and rewritten into the same profile template with invented names and fictional organizations. Product-agnostic: any idea can be run against it in a role session or a five-seat panel run.

Every profile uses the same seven fields — Setting, Tech today, AI stance, Friction, Says yes if / Walks away if, Influence, In their words — at 120 words or fewer including the labels. Seat numbers are permanent; sessions, votes and blockers cite them.

## How the 200 were put together

**Bench A is unchanged from the original 100-persona panel.** Its 30 dentist seats were filled cell by cell against the ADA HPI workforce table scaled to 30 (12 women and 18 men; ages 5 · 8 · 7 · 6 · 4 across the five bands; 22 owners; 10 solo; 5 DSO-affiliated; 12 in Medicaid; six specialists), and its other role splits are informed estimates flagged as such. It carries most of the panel's buying, installing and explaining depth: owners and associates, the office-manager veto, the DSO C-suite and IT, payers, a state board member, a practice-management vendor, a supply representative.

**Bench B keeps what its author built and adds what the template needs.** The imported roster gave each row a role, an organization, tenure, two AI-sentiment columns and a one-line synthesis. Those were kept as the persona's role, years, stance and "In their words". The missing fields — setting size and location, software, the friction moment, the says-yes and walks-away conditions, influence — were written to fit the template, with related rows given a shared fictional employer so colleagues argue from the same building. Forty-six names changed (41 that were real people or fictional characters, 5 that duplicated a Bench A name); four real organizations became fictional ones; two rows whose columns had shifted in the source were restored. Bench B spans 33 states with six rural or small-town settings.

**The dentist mirror at 55 seats, cell by cell.** The HPI table scaled to 55 would be 21 women; ages 9 · 14 · 13 · 10 · 9; 12 specialists; 40 owners; 19 solo; 23 in Medicaid; 9 DSO-affiliated. The merged 55 are 24 women; ages 9 · 18 · 13 · 11 · 4; 18 specialists; 42 owners; 15 solo; 18 in Medicaid; 5 DSO-affiliated. The gaps all come from Bench B's design — a specialist-heavy, owner-heavy, mid-career, suburban provider group with no dentist over 65 and none inside a large DSO — and were left rather than rewritten, because changing a persona's specialty or age to hit a quota would make it someone else. The mirror therefore holds on Bench A; sessions that need it run on Bench A or weight by bench.

**Skeptics.** Bench A seats 35 skeptics, Bench B 18: 53 across the 200, with at least one in every role group of three or more seats. Bench A alone is the harder room.

## How to use the panel, and the caveat

The panel is a casting pool: bring a product or idea, and the personas react to it in a session; nothing here is a reaction to any product.

- **Bind it to a product.** Describe the product in three or four plain sentences (what it does, who uses it, how it is sold; public-level detail only). Then run role sessions, one role at a time: every persona gives a first reaction of up to 40 words, a vote (pursue / pursue with changes / don't pursue) and the one thing that would flip the vote. Tally by role, then roll up: role · tally · first objection · cheapest flip · answer in the next iteration (yes / no / later). The single blocker for that product is named there, not here.
- **Choose the bench by the question.** Bench A alone when the dentist mirror matters (a workforce-representative vote on price, ownership, Medicaid or DSO affiliation) or when the room should be hard: it holds 35 of the 53 skeptics. Both benches when the question needs breadth: 30 patients, 24 DSO functions including compliance and analytics, 16 payer seats including underwriting and appeals, and eleven public-health and academic seats. Every roll-up row says which bench it ran on, and a 200-seat count is reported as two benches side by side, never as one average, because the benches were built to different rules.
- **Split the dentists by cohort in every session,** because the person who signs today and the person who will use it for twenty years are different people. Bench A: up to 10 years out — seats 1, 4, 6, 10, 12, 15, 16, 25; 11 to 25 years — 5, 7, 8, 11, 14, 18, 20, 22, 23, 26, 27, 28; 26 years and more — 2, 3, 9, 13, 17, 19, 21, 24, 29, 30. Bench B: up to 10 years — 103, 106, 109, 110, 112, 115, 122, 124; 11 to 25 — 101, 102, 104, 105, 108, 111, 114, 116, 117, 118, 119, 120, 121, 125; 26 and more — 107, 113, 123. Bench B is mid-career heavy and has three seats in the oldest cohort, so a signers-versus-users gap shows more reliably on Bench A. When the youngest cohort votes no and the oldest votes yes, say so in its own line.
- **Cast a five-seat panel for a sharper argument:** one who pays, one who delivers, one who receives, one who approves, one skeptic — with the channel's blocker in the skeptic seat, from either bench, one named trade-off argued from both sides, votes with flip conditions, and a decision memo.
- **Keep the fields honest.** Any number, rule or capability a persona asserts in a session gets a [check] tag; the 12 [to verify] tags in the profiles and every [check] tag from a session go through a claim audit before anything is reused.
- **Re-cast when the facts change:** sourced figures for hygiene, assisting, front desk, DSO leadership or investors replace the estimated splits; a new product gets its own session, not a rewritten roster. A roster imported from elsewhere is merged the way Bench B was — rewritten into the template, renamed where it names real people or organizations, its author's roles and stances kept, and its deviations from the mirror flagged rather than fixed.

**Caveat.** Two hundred fictional composites with invented names, rehearsing the rooms a dental technology has to walk into. A rehearsal, not evidence: nothing a persona says is quotable as stakeholder data, and the only numbers here that came from a source are the ADA HPI figures behind Bench A's dentist seats.
